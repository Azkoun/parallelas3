# Offers lens (round 3)

## D2: Score 7/10 | Vote: FUND IF

**Strong**
- **Market.** The pain is huge: the cash is already earned and then lost. The buyers have money, and they never pay up front. They are easy to find: one emirate holds a licensed, listable set of outpatient groups, and the doctor network reaches more. The market is growing: health insurance is mandatory and claim volumes rise each year. This passes all four market tests better than any idea so far.
- **Value equation.** The dream outcome is money back that was written off, which is concrete. Time to a first result is 7 days to see the recoverable number. Effort is one read-only export with no integration. Risk is zero: "nothing recovered, nothing owed" builds the guarantee into the price. Saying no feels foolish, which is the point.
- The name, the one-sentence promise and the single entry product are clear.

**Top 3 killers**
1. **The appeal window may already be closed.** "Last year's denials" only works if insurers still accept resubmissions that late. A 2019 UAE RCM rules document sets resubmission at [21 days from remittance](https://www.scribd.com/document/681345053/RCM-RULES). Its current status is unverified. The new Dubai Claims Management Policy Directive (effective 16 Nov 2025) sets [timelines and late-resubmission fees](https://www.tamimi.com/news/dubai-issues-comprehensive-overhaul-of-health-insurance-claims-management-framework/). If most of a 12-month backlog is time-barred, the headline offer promises cash that cannot be recovered, and credibility dies at the first audit. This is the biggest risk to the "likelihood" part of the value equation.
2. **Hidden effort.** Appeals need clinical notes, prior approvals and corrected codes. The export is easy. Gathering the documents is work for clinic staff that the offer does not mention. If clinic staff end up doing the work, the "low effort" claim breaks.
3. **20% of a small claim.** Outpatient denials are often small per claim. Whether 20% covers the human time on each appeal is unknown (open question, /founder-cfo). So is the realistic win rate per insurer, which no one has measured yet. A second worry: clinic owners may fear upsetting insurers they depend on (open question, /founder-consumer).

**Conditions**
- Confirm the current resubmission and appeal windows for each emirate and the main insurers before any pitch.
- Recover cash on 3 real clinics' files, done by hand if needed, so the pitch has a named result.
- Unit cost per appeal is known (/founder-cfo).

**The single change to reach 8+.** Rebuild the entry offer around denials that are still inside the appeal window. Pitch: "send us every denial from the last window; we recover what's still claimable, then work every new denial monthly, same 20%, nothing recovered, nothing owed." The old backlog stays as a free diagnostic: what you lost and why. The promise then matches what can legally be recovered. It also turns a one-off audit into recurring contingency revenue and feeds the dataset every month. With the window verified and one documented recovery, this is an 8.

## E2: Score 6/10 | Vote: FUND IF

**Strong**
- **Time** is excellent: results by tomorrow morning. **Effort** is near zero: upload the files.
- The pain is real. Missing a liquidated-damages, back-to-back or approved-vendor clause before bidding can wipe out a job's margin. $1.5k-$5k per tender is small next to that risk, and the price could plausibly be doubled against a contracts lawyer.
- The Lebanese estimator network makes the buyers easy to reach.

**Top 3 killers**
1. **Likelihood, and the guarantee doesn't fit the risk.** The buyer is trusting the AI's *completeness* across thousands of pages, and there is no proof yet. The guarantee refunds the fee if the matrix is late or incomplete. But the real risk is a clause the matrix *missed*, which is found months later and can cost millions. A refund of $3k does not cover a risk worth millions, so it does not lower the perceived risk.
2. **Market growth is in doubt for the named niche.** "Saudi giga-projects" is the headline market, but it is being cut back. The Line is [reported scaled back and frozen in large sections](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/saudi-arabias-neom-hits-reset-are-costs-for-the-line-spiraling-out-of-control-1.500205617), with [contracts cancelled](https://www.intellinews.com/saudi-arabia-s-neom-cancels-major-trojena-ski-village-433415/). The offer is aimed at a market that may be shrinking.
3. **Money and willingness to pay.** MEP subcontractors are squeezed on cash and payment terms. Whether they pay outside firms for bid work, or see it as estimator time they already pay for, is unknown (open question, /founder-consumer; tender volume per firm, /founder-competitors).

**Conditions**
- A human contracts engineer signs off every matrix.
- Name the target packages beyond NEOM (other PIF, ROSHN, Expo/airport, UAE and Qatar) and their tender volume.
- Know how often firms bid, which decides whether the monthly plan is realistic (/founder-cfo).

**The single change to reach 8+.** Put proof before the purchase with a free back-test. The firm sends a tender it has already bid. BidDesk returns the matrix, and the firm checks it against what its own team found, so the buyer measures likelihood themselves. Then use a guarantee that fits the risk. Example: "find a material clause we missed and that tender is free, plus the next one." The other conditions (sign-off, target packages, bid volume) still apply. With the back-test and that guarantee in place, it reaches 8.

## My pick

**D2, ClaimBack Recovery.** Of the four market tests, it is stronger on money and on growth than E2. Its value equation is close to ideal: a visible number in days, little effort and no risk to the buyer. The hole is factual (the appeal window), not structural, and it can be fixed by rewording the offer. E2's hole is trust, and only months of proof can fill it. Neither score of 8 is earned yet. D2 is one verified fact and one recovered case away from it.
