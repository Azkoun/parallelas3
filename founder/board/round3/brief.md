# Board brief, round 3: two finalists rebuilt to clear 8/10

## The founder
Lebanese, early-stage. Wants a high-ticket AI business sold to companies, investor appeal, and scale beyond Lebanon.
Advantage: Lebanese network across Lebanon and the diaspora (Gulf, West Africa, Europe), Lebanese/Gulf-intelligible Arabic,
French, English. Budget, team, technical skill: NOT given (open questions; do not invent).

## How we got here
Round 1 (owner AI chief of staff, AI collections, WhatsApp ordering) topped out at 6.7. Round 2 tested four ideas;
the best were ClaimBack (6.7) and BidDesk (6.0 average, but 7 on the Monopoly lens). All three lenses said the same thing:
each idea bundled 2-4 products; it needed ONE entry product with a result in days, zero risk to the buyer, a niche the funded
players do not own, and a dataset that compounds with every customer. Both finalists below were rebuilt to those notes.

Known competitors (from round 2 research, linked in round 2 memos):
- Claims/RCM in the Gulf: Klaim (Abu Dhabi/Dubai; claims automation plus receivables financing; reported 100+ facilities in UAE/KSA;
  reported ~$10M Series A + $16M financing), TachyHealth (AI medical coding; $5M Series A led by insurer Tawuniya),
  Santechture (backed by CorroHealth, Gulf Capital, Shorooq; AI RCM), Axora AI (pre-submission denial prediction, UAE/Qatar/KSA).
  These mostly sell to hospitals and focus on submission, coding and financing.
- Gulf construction tech: TruBuild (tender evaluation for project OWNERS), BRKZ (materials procurement, $31M), Builtop,
  ProTenders (tender intelligence/leads). Mostly owner/procurement side; the contractor-side bid desk looked less owned (unverified).
- Reported Gulf claim denial rates: 14% (UAE, 2018), "up to 25% of claim value" (Saudi), up to 30% first-pass at some UAE
  insurers. Old trade-press figures; no current regulator number found.

## Finalist D2: "ClaimBack Recovery"
- One sentence: "Send us the claims insurers denied you last year; we appeal the winnable ones and keep 20% of what we get back.
  Nothing recovered, nothing owed."
- Entry product (only this, at launch): a retrospective denied-claims recovery audit. The clinic gives a read-only export of
  12 months of denials plus remittance files. Within 7 days it sees the recoverable amount per insurer with appeals already
  drafted; humans plus AI resubmit and chase. No integration, no setup fee.
- Niche the funded players ignore: outpatient clinic groups (dental, physiotherapy, dermatology, polyclinics) in ONE emirate
  first, then Lebanese-owned and Lebanese-run clinics across the GCC through the doctor network. Not big hospitals.
- Moat: a per-insurer, per-denial-code dataset of which appeal arguments and documents actually win, updated with every case
  across every client. It gets better with each customer, and it tells future clients exactly what is recoverable.
- Expansion (only after audits prove cash): a pre-submission checker sold on the audit's own numbers; then larger facilities and other GCC countries.
- Price: 15-20% contingency on recovered cash; checker later as a monthly fee per facility.

## Finalist E2: "BidDesk Matrix"
- One sentence: "Drop in the tender; by tomorrow morning get the compliance matrix and the 20 riskiest clauses, or it's free."
- Entry product (only this, at launch): for MEP (mechanical, electrical, plumbing) subcontractors bidding Saudi giga-project
  and large Gulf packages: upload the tender package (specs, contract, particular conditions, often thousands of pages), get
  within 24h a complete compliance matrix (every requirement, where it is, comply/deviate/clarify) and a ranked risk-clause
  report (liquidated damages, payment terms, back-to-back, design responsibility, approved-vendor restrictions) with
  suggested clarifications. Proposal drafting and BOQ pricing are NOT offered until the matrix is trusted.
- Moat: a counterparty rules library: a structured, continuously updated map of each major Gulf project owner's contract
  forms, amendments, typical risk clauses, approved vendor lists and evaluation habits. It compounds across every tender and
  every customer without exposing any customer's prices.
- Distribution: Lebanese estimators, bid managers and MEP firm owners are a dense network in Gulf construction.
- Price: per tender ($1.5k-$5k depending on package size) or a monthly plan ($5k-$15k) for firms bidding often; guarantee: late or incomplete = free.
- Expansion: main contractors, other trades, other Gulf countries, then proposal drafting and pricing support.
