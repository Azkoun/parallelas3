# Offers lens

The Offers lens judges the market first (big pain, buyers with money, easy to reach, growing), then the value equation (dream outcome x likelihood, divided by time and effort), then whether the offer can be made so strong that buyers feel stupid saying no. No market sizes, CAC or competitor data exist yet. Those gaps are marked as open questions below.

## Candidate A: Owner's Eye | Score 6 | Vote: FUND IF

**What is strong.** Buyers with real money and a real, emotional pain: an absent owner losing cash he cannot see. The founder can reach this buyer through his network. Dream outcome is high ("know everything, stop the bleeding, from Dubai"). A success fee on recovered leakage is the natural guarantee, and the ticket ($25k-$100k plus a retainer) fits the founder's goal.

**Top 3 killers**
1. **Likelihood is unproven, and the setup asks for a lot.** "Leakage found" depends on clean data the business does not have (cash logs, Excel, voice notes). Until there is a case study with a dollar figure, the buyer is paying $25k+ on faith.
2. **Effort and trust.** The product has to read staff WhatsApp groups and cash records. Managers, and often family members, will resist being watched. The deployment can stall inside the client for political reasons, not technical ones.
3. **It is a consultancy dressed as software.** 4-8 weeks on-site with forward-deployed engineers caps how many clients the founder can serve and weakens investor appeal. Each client is a custom build.

**Questions for the founder.** What is the first leak you can prove in under 14 days? Who inside the client agrees to share the data, and who loses from it? Can you name three owners who would pay $25k today?

**Conditions (FUND IF).** A 2-week paid "leak audit" (fixed price, fixed deliverable: the top 5 leaks with dollar values) before any full setup. At least 2 audits must find leakage worth more than the setup fee.

## Candidate B: Collect | Score 8 | Vote: FUND

**What is strong.** This is the best market of the three. The pain is massive and gets worse in a cash-starved, post-2019 economy, where an unpaid invoice is often the difference between paying suppliers or not. The buyer has money because the product goes after money he is already owed. Distributors are easy to list and reach through chambers and networks, and they look the same in Lebanon, the Gulf and West Africa.

On the value equation:
- **Dream outcome:** cash in the bank, measured in dollars.
- **Likelihood:** a success fee lets you prove it.
- **Time:** first collections can land within days.
- **Effort:** low if it runs on their existing Excel aging report.

You can guarantee it outright: "no collections, no success fee". That is the strongest offer shape this lens knows.

**Top 3 killers**
1. **Fear for the relationship.** Distributors protect their retailer accounts. If one AI call insults a big customer, the owner switches it off. Escalation rules and tone have to be perfect from day one.
2. **Cash and messy reconciliation.** In Lebanon many payments are collected in cash by drivers or salesmen. If "collected" cannot be matched to "paid", the success fee becomes a fight.
3. **Rules on automated debt collection, plus the price benchmark.** Regulation on automated collection calls in the Gulf and West Africa is unknown. It is also unknown whether 1-3% plus $2k-$5k a month is cheap or expensive next to agencies and in-house collectors. /founder-competitors should answer what collection agencies and existing tools charge. /founder-consumer should answer whether owners trust an AI with their customers.

**Questions for the founder.** What share of a typical distributor's book is more than 90 days overdue? Who chases it today, and what does that cost? Will an owner hand over his worst accounts first?

## Candidate C: Order Desk | Score 6 | Vote: PASS (for now)

**What is strong.** It is a real daily workflow with high volume. Distributors with thousands of retail clients are reachable, and replacing handwritten and phoned-in orders saves measurable time.

**Top 3 killers**
1. **The dream outcome is soft.** "Orders taken more cheaply" is a cost saving, not new money, and it is harder to put a dollar figure on than collected cash. Buyers pay most for outcomes they can count.
2. **Salesmen do more than take orders.** They also handle relationships, shelf space and often cash collection. Replacing them threatens internal politics and may hurt sales. The deployment effort, including ERP integration and price lists, is heavy.
3. **Commodity risk.** WhatsApp ordering bots are likely a crowded category. If the only edge is dialect, this ends up competing on price. /founder-competitors should map this.

**Questions for the founder.** Does any distributor measure what one order costs today? Would a % of order volume survive their margin? /founder-cfo should check it.

## My pick: B (Collect)

Collect wins on the first test this lens applies, the market. The pain is acute, the money is the buyer's own, the buyers are easy to list and reach, and the problem is the same in every country the founder named. It is also the only candidate where the guarantee pays for itself: if it collects nothing, it charges nothing. That makes the likelihood problem go away, which is the problem that sinks A, and it gives the buyer a dollar figure, which C lacks. The 30-120 day credit culture means every distributor has a pile of old receivables, so the first result can come within weeks. It is also more productised than A, which helps the investor story.

**The strongest version:** "Dead Book Recovery."
- **Start where the owner has nothing to lose:** receivables more than 90 days overdue that he has already written off in his head. Relationship risk is lowest there, and any recovery is pure upside.
- **Offer:** setup fee credited against the first collections. Guarantee: if we do not recover X% of the dead book in 90 days, the setup fee comes back. Run 5 founding-client slots per country; the scarcity is real because the team is small.
- **Stack:**
  - Cleanup of the aging report on day 1
  - Dialect agents for Lebanese, Gulf and French
  - Owner-set rules on tone and payment plans
  - A promise-to-pay ledger
  - A reconciliation module for cash collected by drivers
  - Hand-off to a human within the hour
  - A daily voice brief to the owner ("you got $41k in today, here is who promised what"). This borrows A's best feature.
- **Expansion:** move up to current receivables, then retailer credit scoring, then order-taking (C). The founder lands with cash and expands into the whole distributor relationship.

Open before funding: price benchmarks and competitors (/founder-competitors), owner trust and willingness to pay (/founder-consumer), whether 1-3% plus the monthly fee covers delivery and support costs (/founder-cfo).
