# Offers lens (round 2)

Facts used (linked): reported denial/rejection rates in the Gulf range from 14% (UAE, 2018, [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/dubais-hospitals-feel-pain-of-insurance-delays-and-rejections-1.64623975)) to "up to 25% of claim value" (Saudi, [Saudi Gazette](https://www.saudigazette.com.sa/article/524738)), with first-pass rejection at some UAE insurers as high as 30% ([InsuranceMarket.ae](https://insurancemarket.ae/health-insurance/health-insurance-claim-rejection-uae/)). Most figures are old trade-press numbers; no current regulator figure was found. Funded players exist: Klaim (Dubai, reported $10M Series A plus $16M financing, 100+ facilities in UAE/KSA, [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/emea/uae-s-klaim-raises-16m-part-plans-expand-ksa-and-us), [CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Business/UAE-Based-Healthtech-Firm-Klaim-Raises-26M-For-AI-Powered-Payments)) and TachyHealth (AI coding, $5M Series A led by insurer Tawuniya, [Yespress](https://yespress.io/tachyhealth)).

## Candidate D (ClaimBack): Score 7 | Vote: FUND IF

**Strong.** It has the best market of the four. The pain is large and already in money terms: a denied claim is cash the hospital has billed and not received. Buyers have budgets (private hospital groups in mandatory-insurance markets) and are easy to name (CFO / RCM head), and insurance coverage is growing. Dream outcome: "recover cash you have already written off." Recovered cash can be measured, so success pricing and a real guarantee ("no recovery, no fee") fit naturally.

**Top 3 killers.**
1. Likelihood/proof: with no case study, a CFO has no reason to believe the AI recovers more than their own billing team. Round 1's "reads as a consultancy" risk comes back if early recovery depends on human appeal writers.
2. Effort: hooking into the hospital information system and insurer portals is heavy work for the buyer. If onboarding takes months of IT work, the value equation collapses.
3. Funded players in the same region. Klaim and TachyHealth sell to the same buyer, and at least one large Saudi group already uses AI on rejections ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/ai-has-dramatically-lowered-insurance-claim-rejections-says-healthcare-group-cfo)). The edge has to be a sharper offer, not "AI for claims."

**+2 change.** Drop the $10k-$40k setup fee and lead with a **zero-risk retrospective audit**: "Give us read-only export of the last 12 months of denied claims; we resubmit/appeal what is winnable and take 15-20% of cash actually collected. Nothing collected, nothing owed." That turns proof into a product (likelihood up), needs one data export instead of an integration (effort down), and pays in weeks (time down). The forward-looking pre-submission checker then becomes the upsell, sold with the audit's own numbers. Pick one country and one payer platform first.

**Conditions.**
- Show that 30-50% of a real sample of denied claims are recoverable, using one friendly facility's data (open question; /founder-consumer for buyer appetite, /founder-cfo for the recovery-rate math).
- Find exactly where Klaim/TachyHealth do not reach: denials appeals vs. pre-submission vs. financing (/founder-competitors).
- Check whether Saudi pre-approval rules are changing ([Gulf Business](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2025/insights/healthcare-in-saudi-arabia-will-insurance-approvals-be-eliminated/)), since a change would shrink part of the pain.

## Candidate E (BidDesk): Score 6 | Vote: FUND IF

**Strong.** The buyers have big budgets (Gulf megaproject contractors), the founder can actually reach them through Lebanese estimators and engineers, and "weeks to days" is a clear time cut.

**Top 3 killers.**
1. The dream outcome cannot be attributed to the product. Winning a bid depends on price, relationships and politics, so a success fee is hard to defend. That leaves a $5k-$25k/month software fee, which is a cost-saving sale, not a revenue sale.
2. Effort: it only works if the firm hands over past bids and rate history, and estimators may resist a tool that threatens their role.
3. Unknown competition: general AI document tools and tender-analysis software may already cover the compliance-matrix step (open question, /founder-competitors).

**+2 change.** Sell a narrow, guaranteed deliverable: "Compliance matrix plus risk-clause report on any tender within 48 hours, or it's free," priced per tender. Prove it on 10 live tenders before building proposal drafting or BOQ pricing.

**Conditions.** Get named estimators to confirm the hours each tender costs today and what they would pay per tender (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo).

## Candidate F (Order Desk Network): Score 5 | Vote: PASS

**Strong.** Used daily, simple to explain, free for retailers.

**Top 3 killers.**
1. Weak market on two of the four checks: Beirut pharmacies and groceries have little money, and the paying side (distributors) is small and not clearly growing.
2. The distributor's dream outcome ("more orders") is vague: the retailer would have ordered anyway, so the product mostly moves existing orders to a new channel. It saves cost, which brings price competition against the round-1 players.
3. The value that could earn an 8 (trade credit) is a second business that needs capital and lending licences. Those are open questions, and right now that value is only a promise.

**+2 change.** Lead with credit, not ordering. Sell distributors "we cut the days your money is stuck with shops (days sales outstanding) and take on the bad-debt risk," priced on cash released, with ordering as the free tool that gathers the data. This needs a funding partner (/founder-cfo).

## Candidate G (TradeDesk): Score 5 | Vote: PASS

**Strong.** "We find duty you overpaid and claim it back" is a recovered-cash outcome that suits a contingency fee. The Lebanese trading-house network is a real way to reach buyers.

**Top 3 killers.**
1. The size of the pain is unknown and may be small. GCC duty rates are mostly low and flat, and refund windows and procedures vary by country. The money available to recover per importer could be too small for a high-ticket offer (open question, /founder-cfo).
2. Likelihood depends on customs authorities paying out. In Lebanon and parts of West Africa, informal practice may outweigh the rules, so the AI's accuracy may not lead to cash.
3. The broker channel conflicts: brokers are paid to do the work this replaces.

**+2 change.** Narrow to one corridor where duty is high and free-trade-agreement rules are complex and enforced (e.g. goods entering a market with real preferential-origin savings). Offer a "duty-savings audit, paid only from refunds" with evidence of refund amounts per importer.

## My pick and the strongest version of it

**D, ClaimBack.** It is the only candidate where the market passes all four checks and the outcome is cash the buyer can count. It is also the only one where a "no recovery, no fee" guarantee costs the founder nothing to offer.

Strongest version, as an offer: **"Denied-Claims Recovery Audit: we recover cash from your last 12 months of denials in 60 days, and you pay only a share of what lands in your bank."**
- Stack: retrospective audit with a dashboard by insurer and reason → appeals written and filed in Arabic/English → a monthly "top 10 denial causes" report for the clinical and coding teams → (upsell) pre-submission checking priced on the rejection-rate drop against the audit's baseline.
- Guarantee: no fee on any claim not collected. Fee ends on pre-submission if first-pass rejection does not fall by an agreed amount in 90 days.
- Honest scarcity: onboard a limited number of facilities per quarter because each audit needs coder review. This only holds if it is true.
- Lebanese edge used correctly: warm introductions to RCM heads at Lebanese-run groups get the first 3 audits, and the audits create the case studies.
- Moat: a growing per-insurer library of denial reasons and winning appeals.

This reaches an 8 only after one real audit shows recovered cash. Until then it is a 7.
