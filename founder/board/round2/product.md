# Product lens (round 2)

The Product lens asks four things of each candidate: what is the one thing, what do the customer's first five minutes feel like, what gets cut, and where does the experience pass between people or systems and break. None of the four earns an 8 as written. Each one bundles two to four products into a single pitch.

## Candidate D: Score 7 | Vote: FUND IF

**What is strong.** The moment of use is concrete and frequent: a claim is about to go out, and the product says "this one will be denied, here is the fix." That happens on every claim, every day, so it is the most-used product of the four. The result is money the CFO can count, and success shows up in the hospital's own remittance data, not in a report the vendor writes. The pitch fits in one sentence: "Stop insurance denials before they happen, and win back the ones that already did."

**Top 3 killers.**
1. *The seam with the hospital's systems.* Checking claims before submission means sitting between the hospital information system and the insurer's submission channel. If staff have to copy claims into a separate tool, they will stop within weeks. How each target country's claims flow works (integration points, file formats, who controls access) is an open question. /founder-competitors should map what existing revenue-cycle vendors integrate with.
2. *Two products in one.* Checking before submission (inside the workflow, needs integration, saves money over time) and appeals for denied claims (works after the fact from denial files, recovers cash) have different users, different data and different first five minutes. Building both at once halves the craft on each.
3. *The quality nobody sees.* Insurer rules change, and a wrong prediction or a weak appeal costs the client money and trust. The rules library has to be kept current per insurer, and the customer never sees that work. If it is not owned and maintained, the product rots without anyone noticing until results drop.

**Change worth +2.** Cut to one country, one segment (clinic groups or hospitals, not both) and one entry product: appeals. The first five minutes are "upload your last 90 days of denials and see the recoverable amount, insurer by insurer, with appeals already drafted." No integration is needed to deliver the first result. Checking before submission becomes the second product, sold to clients who have already seen the recovered cash.

**Conditions.** (a) The founder shows the claims and denial data can be obtained in a usable form, ideally a real anonymised file from one warm-intro facility. (b) A named owner for the per-insurer rules library. (c) Willingness to pay a percentage of recovered cash is tested with /founder-consumer, and whether the economics hold is checked with /founder-cfo.

## Candidate E: Score 5 | Vote: PASS

**What is strong.** The pain is real and easy to picture: weeks of estimators working through thousands of pages. "The compliance matrix in a day instead of a week" is a moment a bid manager would feel. Drafting in the firm's own style from its past bids is a real craft detail.

**Top 3 killers.**
1. *No single thing.* Compliance matrix, clause risk, proposal drafting and BOQ pricing are four products with four users (bid manager, legal, technical writer, estimator). The pitch needs a paragraph.
2. *Used rarely and in bursts.* A firm uses it per tender, not daily, so habit and data build slowly. How many tenders a target firm bids on in a year is an open question for /founder-consumer.
3. *Seams everywhere.* Drawings, scanned specs, BOQs inside estimating software, and historical rates the firm guards closely. Pricing help means touching the firm's most sensitive numbers, and one wrong rate in a won bid is a loss the client will remember.

**Change worth +2.** Cut to one thing: "Drop in the tender, get the compliance matrix and the 20 riskiest clauses by tomorrow morning," for MEP subcontractors only. Drop proposal drafting and pricing until the matrix is trusted.

## Candidate F: Score 7 | Vote: FUND IF

**What is strong.** It has the best first five minutes of the four: a pharmacist sends a voice note in dialect and the order is placed. It is used daily, it fits in one sentence, and the retailer learns nothing new because they already use WhatsApp. This is the only candidate whose product is the habit.

**Top 3 killers.**
1. *The promise depends on the other side.* "Order from ALL your suppliers" is only true once most of a retailer's suppliers are on. Until then the retailer still phones the others, and the product has become one more channel instead of replacing them.
2. *The seam after the voice note.* Stock, price, confirmation, delivery time and substitutions are handled by distributor staff and systems the founder does not control. If a WhatsApp order goes unanswered once, the retailer goes back to the phone. The experience is only as good as the slowest distributor.
3. *Too much scope.* Ordering, a network and trade credit are three businesses. Credit adds underwriting, collections and regulation before the ordering habit is even proven. Round 1 also named funded players nearby (Choco, BeatRoute, Sukhiba, Cravin). Whether they operate in the target city needs /founder-competitors.

**Change worth +2.** Own the confirmation loop. Every order gets a confirmed price, availability and delivery window within a fixed time, or the network escalates it itself. Start with one anchor distributor's whole retailer base in one vertical, and promise "your orders to [anchor] confirmed in minutes" rather than "all suppliers." Remove credit from the pitch.

**Conditions.** One anchor distributor signed before build, a confirmation-time target the founder will publish, and credit deferred until order volume is proven (sized with /founder-cfo).

## Candidate G: Score 6 | Vote: FUND IF

**What is strong.** It is used per shipment, so active importers use it often. "We found duty you overpaid" is a moment with real money in it, and HS classification is a craft problem where precision shows.

**Top 3 killers.**
1. *Four products.* Classification, declarations, FTA eligibility and refund claims. The pitch needs a paragraph.
2. *Seams with national customs systems and brokers.* Every country has its own filing system and procedures, and brokers sit in the middle. Whether brokers welcome or resist a tool that touches their fee is an open question for /founder-consumer.
3. *Errors are costly.* A misclassification brings penalties, so the product needs human sign-off, and that makes it harder to keep simple.

**Change worth +2.** Cut to the refund audit for one country: "Send us last year's declarations and see the duty you overpaid, with the claims drafted." Paid on recovery, with clearance automation offered to clients once the audit has proved accurate.

**Conditions.** That refunds can actually be claimed in the chosen country, the time limits and the typical amounts are open questions, to be checked with /founder-competitors (existing refund specialists) and /founder-cfo (recovery economics).

## My pick and the strongest version of it

**Fund D.** It is the only candidate that combines daily use, a concrete moment of use, money the customer can count in their own data, and an entry product (appeals) that delivers its first result without integration. F has the better habit, but its experience depends on a network that does not exist yet. D depends only on files the hospital already has.

**The strongest version.** One country (the founder's warmest network decides which), one segment (private clinic groups first, since they decide faster than hospitals; to be validated with /founder-consumer), one sentence: *"Upload your denials, get back the money insurers owe you."*

- **First five minutes:** a revenue-cycle head uploads 90 days of denial and remittance files. In a single screen they see recoverable money by insurer and denial reason, with appeals drafted, each needing one click to review.
- **Months 2 to 6:** the same denial reasons, now known for each insurer, become the pre-submission check. The product moves from recovering money to protecting it, priced as a share of both.
- **Cut:** a multi-country launch, hospitals and clinics in the same year, a dashboard full of charts, and anything that needs integration before the first recovered dollar.
- **Own the craft:** a maintained rules library per insurer, appeal letters a billing expert would sign without editing, and a monthly statement of recovered cash that the CFO forwards to the owner.

Open before this can reach 8: who already sells revenue-cycle and denial tools in the chosen country (/founder-competitors), whether buyers accept a percentage of recovered cash (/founder-consumer), and the recovery rate, success-fee economics and setup cost (/founder-cfo).
