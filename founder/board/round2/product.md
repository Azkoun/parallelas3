# Product lens (round 2)

The Product lens asks of each candidate: what is the one thing, what do the customer's first five minutes feel like, what gets cut, and where does the experience pass between people or systems and break. None earns an 8 as written. Each bundles two to four products into one pitch.

## Candidate D: Score 7 | Vote: FUND IF

**What is strong.** A concrete moment that happens on every claim, every day: "this claim will be denied, here is the fix." The result is money the CFO can count in the hospital's own remittance data. It fits in one sentence: "Stop insurance denials before they happen, and win back the ones that already did."

**Top 3 killers.**
1. *The seam with hospital systems.* Checking before submission means sitting between the hospital information system and the insurer's submission channel. If staff must copy claims into a separate tool, they stop within weeks. Integration points per country are an open question (/founder-competitors: what existing revenue-cycle vendors plug into).
2. *Two products in one.* Checking before submission (needs integration, protects money) and appealing denials (works from files, recovers cash) have different users, data and first five minutes.
3. *Quality nobody sees.* Insurer rules change. Unless someone owns and maintains the rules library per insurer, the product decays quietly until results drop.

**Change worth +2.** One country, one segment, one entry product: appeals. First five minutes: "Upload 90 days of denials, see the recoverable amount by insurer, with appeals already drafted." No integration needed for the first result. Checking before submission becomes the second product, sold to clients who have already seen the cash.

**Conditions.** A real anonymised denial file from one warm-intro facility, a named owner for the rules library, and success-fee pricing tested (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo).

## Candidate E: Score 5 | Vote: PASS

**What is strong.** The pain is easy to picture: estimators spending weeks on thousands of pages. "The compliance matrix in a day, not a week" is a moment a bid manager would feel.

**Top 3 killers.**
1. *No one thing.* Compliance matrix, clause risk, proposal drafting and BOQ pricing are four products for four users. The pitch needs a paragraph.
2. *Rare, bursty use.* Used per tender, not daily, so habit and data build slowly. How many tenders a firm bids a year is open (/founder-consumer).
3. *Seams everywhere.* Scanned specs, drawings, estimating software, guarded historical rates. One wrong rate in a won bid is remembered forever.

**Change worth +2.** For MEP subcontractors only: "Drop in the tender, get the compliance matrix and the 20 riskiest clauses by tomorrow morning." Drop drafting and pricing until the matrix is trusted.

## Candidate F: Score 7 | Vote: FUND IF

**What is strong.** The best first five minutes of the four: a pharmacist sends a voice note in dialect and the order is placed. Used daily, one sentence, nothing new to learn. The only candidate where the product is the habit.

**Top 3 killers.**
1. *The promise depends on the other side.* "Order from ALL your suppliers" is false until most suppliers are on. Until then it is one more channel, not a replacement for the phone.
2. *The seam after the voice note.* Stock, price, confirmation and delivery sit with distributor staff and systems the founder does not control. One unanswered order and the retailer goes back to the phone.
3. *Scope.* Ordering, network and trade credit are three businesses. Round 1 named funded players nearby (Choco, BeatRoute, Sukhiba, Cravin). Whether they reach the target city is open (/founder-competitors).

**Change worth +2.** Own the confirmation loop: every order gets a confirmed price, availability and delivery window within a fixed time, or the network escalates it. Start with one anchor distributor's retailer base and promise "your orders to [anchor] confirmed in minutes," not "all suppliers." Remove credit from the pitch.

**Conditions.** Anchor distributor signed before building, a published confirmation-time target, and credit deferred until order volume is proven (/founder-cfo).

## Candidate G: Score 6 | Vote: FUND IF

**What is strong.** Used per shipment, so often for active importers. "We found duty you overpaid" carries real money, and HS classification is a craft where precision shows.

**Top 3 killers.**
1. *Four products.* Classification, declarations, FTA eligibility, refunds.
2. *Seams with national customs systems and brokers.* Each country files differently, and brokers sit in the middle. Whether they adopt or resist it is open (/founder-consumer).
3. *Costly errors.* Misclassification brings penalties, so a human must sign off, which works against simplicity.

**Change worth +2.** Only the refund audit, one country: "Send last year's declarations, see the duty you overpaid, claims drafted." Paid on recovery. Offer clearance automation only once the audit has proved accurate.

**Conditions.** That refunds can be claimed in the chosen country, the time limits and the typical amounts (/founder-competitors for existing refund specialists, /founder-cfo for recovery economics).

## My pick and the strongest version of it

**Fund D.** It is the only candidate with daily use, a concrete moment, money counted in the customer's own data, and an entry product that delivers its first result without integration. F has the better habit, but its experience depends on a network that does not exist yet. D depends only on files the hospital already has.

**Strongest version.** One country (the warmest network decides), private clinic groups first if they decide faster than hospitals (to validate with /founder-consumer), one sentence: *"Upload your denials, get back the money insurers owe you."*

- **First five minutes:** a revenue-cycle head uploads 90 days of denial files. One screen shows recoverable money by insurer and reason, with appeals drafted, one click each to review.
- **Months 2-6:** the denial reasons learned per insurer become the check before submission. The product moves from recovering money to protecting it, priced as a share of both.
- **Cut:** multi-country launch, hospitals and clinics in the same year, chart-heavy dashboards, and anything needing integration before the first recovered dollar.
- **Craft:** a maintained rules library per insurer, appeals a billing expert would sign unedited, and a monthly recovered-cash statement the CFO forwards to the owner.

Open before this can reach 8: who already sells denial tools in the chosen country (/founder-competitors), whether buyers accept a percentage of recovered cash (/founder-consumer), and recovery rate, fee economics and setup cost (/founder-cfo).
