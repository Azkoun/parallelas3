# Product lens

The Product lens asks four things of each candidate: what is the one thing, what do the first five minutes feel like, what would you cut, and where is the experience handed between people or systems (the seams).

## Candidate A: "Owner's Eye" | Score 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

**What is strong.** The best moment of all three candidates is here: a 90-second voice note in Lebanese dialect every morning saying "yesterday the Accra branch sold 40 cartons but stock dropped 55; supplier X's price jumped 12%." That is a product an owner would show his friends. The pain (an absent owner who cannot see where the money goes) is real and emotional.

**Top 3 killers (most dangerous first)**
1. **It is not one thing.** It reads WhatsApp, voice notes, POS, Excel and cash logs, flags four kinds of leak, and later adds a "Founder Twin." The 4-8 week on-site deployment shows it is still a custom consulting project. Every client will be different, so there is no single experience to polish.
2. **Seams everywhere.** Each data source is a hand-off where things break: messy Excel files, cash that never touches a system, WhatsApp groups the staff can leave or move. One false alarm ("your manager is stealing") that turns out to be a data error kills the owner's trust, and possibly a family relationship.
3. **Staff experience.** The people whose chats get read are part of the product too. If they feel watched they will move the real talk off the monitored groups, and the product goes blind without anyone noticing.

**Questions for the founder.** In one sentence, which single leak do you find first? What does day 1 feel like before any integration is done? Who checks a flag before the owner hears it?

**Conditions.** Cut it to one leak type from one data source (for example stock vs sales from the POS), delivered as the daily voice brief. Drop the Founder Twin. Get deployment under one week.

## Candidate B: "Collect" | Score 6/10 | Vote: FUND IF

**What is strong.** It does one clear thing with a number attached: overdue money comes in. The one-sentence pitch works ("we get your retailers to pay what they owe, on WhatsApp, in their dialect"). The outcome shows up in the bank, so it is easy to prove.

**Top 3 killers**
1. **The person on the other end does not want this product.** The retailer being chased is the distributor's customer, and in Lebanese trade the relationship *is* the business. One badly judged message (wrong amount, chasing an invoice that was already paid in cash, a tone that insults an old client) costs more than the debt. The owner will feel that risk and keep the agent on a short leash.
2. **The reconciliation seam.** Much of the payment is dollar cash handed to a driver or salesman, recorded late or in Excel. If the ledger is wrong, the agent chases people who have already paid. The product is only as good as data it does not control.
3. **Negotiation is judgment.** "Payment plans within rules the owner sets" assumes owners can write the rules down. Most decide case by case based on family, history and the next order. Escalations to a human could become the main workflow.

**Questions for the founder.** What does the retailer see in the first message? How do you know an invoice is still unpaid today? What happens when the agent is wrong?

**Conditions.** Start with reminders and promise-to-pay logging only, with no negotiation. Do not launch until reconciliation is correct, including cash. Every message gets owner-approved templates.

## Candidate C: "Order Desk" | Score 8/10 | Vote: FUND

**What is strong.** It has a clear "one thing" and a user who *wants* it. The moment: a pharmacist sends a voice note at 9pm ("send me two boxes of the usual and the new syrup"). Within seconds she gets back a clean list with prices, stock and a delivery day, plus one useful suggestion. She taps confirm, and it lands in the ERP. That happens daily for thousands of retailers, so it is easy to see whether it works and easy to polish. Both sides gain: the retailer gets speed and the distributor gets clean orders.

**Top 3 killers**
1. **A wrong order is a visible failure.** Dialect voice notes, brand nicknames, units ("carton," "dozen," "the usual"), mixed Arabic/French/English. A misheard order means a wrong delivery, a return and a retailer back on the phone to the salesman. Accuracy is the product.
2. **The stock/price seam.** If ERP stock or price lists are stale, the agent confidently confirms things that cannot ship. The confirmation is a promise the distributor's warehouse has to keep.
3. **The salesman's role is bigger than taking orders.** Salesmen also collect cash, check shelves and hold the relationship. If they see the product as a threat they will steer retailers away from it. The product has to make the salesman's day better, not just replace him.

**Questions for the founder.** What is the order accuracy target, and what happens on a low-confidence line? Does the agent ask back, or hand off to a human? How fresh is the stock data at a typical distributor?

**Open questions (not answered here).** Who already sells WhatsApp ordering to distributors in Lebanon, the Gulf and West Africa? */founder-competitors*. Will retailers actually switch from the salesman or the phone? */founder-consumer*. Does setup plus monthly fee, or a percentage of volume, pay back at a pilot distributor's order count? */founder-cfo*.

## My pick: C, "Order Desk"

Through the Product lens, C is the only candidate where the end user is pleased to use the product, where the core moment happens many times a day, and where the one thing ("send a voice note, get a confirmed order") fits in one sentence. A asks you to master five data sources before the experience exists. B builds its experience on top of the most uncomfortable conversation in the client's business. C can be made excellent and measured every day: accuracy, time to confirm, reorders through the channel.

**The strongest version.** Start with one distributor in one vertical (pharma or FMCG in Lebanon) and do one thing perfectly: dialect voice note to confirmed order in under 30 seconds, with a human checking anything below a confidence threshold so no wrong order ever ships. Give salesmen a dashboard of their retailers' orders so they become account managers rather than order-takers. Once the WhatsApp thread is the retailer's habit, that same thread is the natural home for B's features (statements, gentle payment reminders) and later for A's insight (what is selling, where). Earn the right to the other two candidates by owning this one experience first.
